Who I Am

Not a job title. A way of paying attention.

I got into product management by accident and stayed for the obsession: watching someone use something I helped build, and seeing the exact moment it clicks — or doesn't. That moment is where I live.

I've spent my career moving between very different worlds — luxury real estate, SaaS hiring platforms, fintech, casino gaming — but the job is always the same underneath: sit with the customer's confusion, argue for what they actually need (not what's easiest to ship), and rally engineers, designers and executives around a version of the product that earns trust.

Lately I'm most alive working on AI — not as a buzzword, but as the thing that finally lets a product answer a customer the way a great salesperson would, at 2am, in seconds. That's what I'm building into Aldar's digital ecosystem right now.

How I Work

What I actually believe about building products

The customer is not a persona

Personas are a starting guess. I'd rather sit in on ten real sales calls than read one slide of assumptions — the confusion people show you is more honest than the answers they give in interviews.

Immersive isn't a gimmick

VR tours and immersive web experiences only earn their place if they shorten the distance between "I'm curious" and "I'm confident." I've killed features that were impressive and kept ones that were just useful.

AI should feel like help, not a demo

The bar for an AI assistant isn't "does it work" — it's "would I trust it with a $2M decision?" I build for that bar, which usually means saying no to the flashy version.

Numbers earn the argument

I love a good instinct, but in the room with executives, GA4 and Mixpanel do the persuading. Data doesn't replace judgment — it's what makes judgment hard to dismiss.
